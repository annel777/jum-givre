At the counter everyone talks fast: "one scoop or two?", "cone or cup?", "any
toppings?". Here are the words, one at a time, so ordering stays fun instead of
feeling like a test.

## Ice cream, custard, sorbet: not the same thing

**Ice cream** is the umbrella word, the one we use for everything. Underneath it sit
two clearly different families.

**Dairy ice cream** is made with milk or cream, often egg yolks. It is what gives you
vanilla, chocolate, pistachio, caramel. It is soft in the mouth and melts leaving a
rich film on the tongue.

**Sorbet** has no dairy at all: water, sugar and a lot of fruit. It feels colder,
sharper, more acidic. Lemon, raspberry, mango and passion fruit are almost always
sorbets. It is also the default choice when you avoid dairy.

<ConseilGlacier>
  A well-made sorbet is the colour of its fruit, no more. A neon-pink raspberry or a
  screaming-yellow lemon has usually had some help.
</ConseilGlacier>

## Gelato: the Italian one

**Gelato** is Italian ice cream — Italian as in the country, not as in the machine. Two
differences from classic ice cream: less air is whipped in while it churns, and it is
served slightly less cold. The result is a denser, more supple texture, and a flavour
you catch straight away because the cold is not numbing your tongue.

That is what <LienFiche slug="gelato-junkie" /> sells, and it is why those scoops taste
stronger for the same quantity.

## Soft serve: here the machine does the work

Careful with the trap: in France *glace à l'italienne* does not mean gelato, it means
**soft serve** — the swirl that comes out of a machine, light, full of air, served
immediately. It is the seaside and funfair classic.

It rarely comes as scoops: it arrives in a cone or a cup, waiting for its toppings.
<LienFiche slug="my-boule" /> works this way, which is why its review gives a price per
cup rather than per scoop.

## Scoop, cup, cone: how it reaches your hand

A **scoop** is the reference portion: a scoop dipped into the tub and rolled. Shops
price per scoop, and you add scoops.

A **cone** is the wafer holder. You eat it, it crunches, and it leaks fast in the sun.

A **cup** is the cardboard tub with its little flat spoon. Less photogenic, far more
practical when you are walking.

Those three words matter in this guide: you cannot compare the price of a scoop with
the price of a cup, so every review says what its price buys.

## Sundae: when ice cream becomes a dessert

A **sundae** is ice cream served in a tall glass, covered with a sauce (chocolate,
caramel, red fruit), almost always with whipped cream, and something crunchy on top. It
was born in the United States at the end of the 19th century, in soda fountains —
several American towns still claim to have invented it.

What makes it different from a plain scoop is not the quantity, it is the intention: a
sundae is eaten sitting down, with a spoon, and it is built in layers.

## Topping: everything that lands on top

A **topping** is what you add over the ice cream: chocolate sauce, fruit coulis, chips,
chopped hazelnuts, marshmallows, sweets, crumbled biscuit, whipped cream. Some shops
include them, others charge per topping — worth asking before saying yes to everything.

<ConseilGlacier>
  Warm sauce on very cold ice cream: the contrast is the whole point. But it melts
  things fast, so ask for it at the last moment.
</ConseilGlacier>

## A few more words you will hear

- **Granita**: flavoured crushed ice that you scrape. It is not a sorbet, it is
  coarser, and it is meant to be half drunk.
- **Flavour**: what is in one tub.
- **Display case**: the chilled cabinet. Open cases with full, gently domed tubs, or
  closed cases where the ice cream is hidden: both exist, and the second is not
  suspicious at all, quite the opposite.
- **Tub**: the stainless steel pan holding one flavour.

With these ten words you can order anywhere in Cannes without hesitating — and read the
reviews without wondering why the twins talk about cups in one shop and scoops in
another.
